This short article describes a new idea by James Hartness, of Detroit, for a balloon that prevents accidents due to bursting. "Instead of making a single globe, he constructs the body of the balloon in sections, exactly similar to those of an orange, each one of which is inflated separately, and all joined together complete the sphere." Figure 1 below shows several sections of the balloon joined together, and the second figure shows one of the small sections. The sections are held together by straps, depicted as *b* in Figure 1. A rope ladder extends from the top of the balloon to the bottom, and the aeronaut can use it to access the valves in each of the sections.

Hartness argues that the fabric for this particular balloon can be lighter because each section only needs to withstand a small amount of pressure. Furthermore, any rupture is confined to only a small section, so the balloon can remain in the air because of the bouyancy of the other, uninjured sections.
