In 1911, American jockey Tod Sloan went into partnership with a man named Clancey and opened the New York Bar in Paris. Harry MacElhone, a bartender, took over the bar in 1923 and changed the name to Harry's New York Bar. The following year, MacElhone and Oscar Odd McIntyre, a columnist, founded the International Bar Flies (IBF), an organization "devoted to the uplift and downfall of serious drinkers." The front of the IBF membership booklet is shown below. The booklet contains rules, the IBF song, and a list of watering holes around the world that welcome IBF members.

*Barflies and Cocktails* was printed in 1927 and contains over 300 recipes for drinks along with many humorous illustrations and advertisements. It was written by MacElhone and journalist/author D. B. Wyndham Lewis, with contributions from Arthur Moss (who wrote the "Around the Town" column in the New York Herald in Paris). In the preface, MacElhone states that the purpose of this "pocket Cocktail guide" is to "fill every requirement," whether it's drink recipes, valuable hints, or toasts. Following it will allow "the veriest novice in the business to become an expert mixer and a thoroughly competent bar-tender." The recipes are arranged in alphabetical order, and many of the pages contain illustrations that go along with some of the cocktails.

The back of the book contains a discussion on the beginnings of the IBF, a list of the first 500 members of the IBF, the code of the bar flies, a list of toasts, and some notes on wine. At the very front and very back of the book are two photographs from Harry's New York Bar. This copy also comes with a loose chart called "Here's How," which is a small guide to preparing 26 "very delightful, tested cocktails."
