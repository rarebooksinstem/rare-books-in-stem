These notebooks were owned by two individuals in 1840s America and contain notes on arithmetic, geometry, among other topics. 

{{< figure src="notebooks.jpg" title="Notebooks of Charles Tilghman Yeager and John Carpenter" width="550" class="left">}}

The first notebook was owned by Charles Tilghman Yeager of Upper Saucon Township in Lehigh County, Pennsylvania. According to History of Lehigh County, Pennsylvania, Charles was born on January 11, 1824. He worked as a farmer his entire life and was a "well-read man." His notebook begins in 1841, and all of the entries that year are about topics like bartering and calculating taxes. The next set of entries begin on 1848 and are about commonwealth laws about land ownership. It is unclear if he was simply copying passages from other books or trying to write something new. Then there is a gap until 1855, when there is a list of customers, what they purchased, and how much they paid. This continues until April of 1893 and picks up again on April 14, 1910. Given that he died on April 4, 1910, it is very possible that the notebook was used for another year by someone else to keep track of customers. Below are some samples of the notes Yeager took.

The second notebook was owned by a John Carpenter and contains geometry problems. Based on the date next to his name, it is assumed he acquired this notebook on January 18, 1842. It is also assumed that he was about 16 when he started taking these notes based on the subject matter. Given how common his name is and the fact that there is nothing in the notebook to indicate where he lived, I have been unable to find anything conclusive about him.
