*Selenographical Journal* is an extremely rare journal that was created by amateur astronomer William Radcliffe Birt (1804-1881) to advance the field of selenography. Articles and observations by various authors/astronomers/researchers were published monthly. One of the main goals was to create a more accurate and complete lunar map. Birt died in December of 1881, but the journal continued publishing papers for another year. His death had a great impact on the journal's ability to continue. In the January 1882 issue, there is some discussion about raising more money for the Selenographical Society and also about possibly reducing the publication of the journal from monthly to quarterly.

As stated by Birt in the first issue, "The object of this monthly journal is to secure the co-operation of the various students of the lunar surface. It is intended to contain, in addition to the various *Ephemerides* giving such data as the position of the terminator, geocentric librations, &c., for days on which the Moon can be observed, certain diagrams of objects to which the attention of the members of the society will be from time to time directed, with instructions for the making of such observations as will be most valuable for the advancement of Lunar knowledge. Great advantages too are expected to be gained by the adoption of a standard nomenclature, and scale of brightness. The Editors will be glad to receive any queries or short notes connected with Lunar Topography."

This copy includes all fifty-nine issues across five volumes (bound as four). The first issue is dated April 2nd, 1878, and the last issue is dated December dec 10, 1882. The front pastedown of the first volume contains the label of William Wesley & Son, Booksellers & Publishers.
