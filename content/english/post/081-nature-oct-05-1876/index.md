On August 28th of 1876, the Count Elemer Bathyani (Batthy&#225;ny) and the balloonist Claude-Jules Duruof made an ascent in the balloon "Tricolore," which was decorated in the red, white, and blue colors of the French flag. The ascent began at La Villette Gasworks at 11:50 in the morning and ended at two in the afternoon about forty miles outside of Paris. The balloon ascended to a height of 2,500 meters.

This article by French balloonist Wilfrid de Fonvielle describes the experiments conducted by Bathyani during this ascent. Bathyani used a radiometer constructed by the company Etablissements Gaiffe. His goals were "to rotate the radiometer at different altitudes, so as to illustrate the augmentation of the luminosity of the sun, and to condense the vapour of clouds with an ether evaporator, in order to collect moleculae suspended in the air, and ascertain whether vapour was mixed with a certain quantity of ammoniac, nitrous or nitric compounds, or ozone." It is noted that the latter experiment could not be conducted because "the balloon did not meet a true cloud." The results of the other experiments are given.

de Fonvielle ends by saying that Adolphe Gaiffe was constructing "another differential radiometer to rotate under similar circumstances." Some design parameters of the radiometer are given.
