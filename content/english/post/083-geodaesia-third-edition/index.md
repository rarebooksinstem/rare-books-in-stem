First printed in 1688, *Geodaesia; Or, the Art of Surveying and Measuring of Land Made Easie* was written by John Love as a way to make surveying more accessible to students in the field. The work is divided into 13 chapters and includes discussions on arithmetic, geometry, the use of instruments, practical surveying, trigonometry, and calculating heights and distances. There are numerous mathematical diagrams, illustrations, and tables throughout the book.

{{< figure src="title page.jpg" title="Title page of the third edition" width="550" class="left">}}

The end of the preface contains an advertisement for instruments sold by John Rowley (1666-1728) at his shop on Fleet Street in London. Rowley is best known for his orrery, or mechanical model of the solar system, which got its name from a model Rowley created for his patron Charles Boyle, 4th Earl of Orrery. After the table of contents and before the start of the first chapter is an additional advertisement for mathematics books printed for William Taylor.

{{< figure src="advertisements.jpg" title="Advertisement for mathematical works printed for William Taylor" width="550" class="left">}}

The first edition was the only edition printed in the 17th century. A second edition appeared in 1715. Several editions of this work appeared throughout the 18th century, with the 13th and final edition appearing in 1796. The 12th and 13th editions were printed in New York. *Geodaesia* appears to be the only work Love wrote.
