This scarce first edition of John Webster's *Elements of Natural Philosophy* was written as a "humble endeavour ... to collect and methodize those demonstrative truths, which have been drawn from the bosom of Nature by the deep researches of the philosopher, and to render them plain and evident to those, whose time and education will not enable them to draw their information from original sources." In the preface, Webster states that half of young people in public schools need only "a general knowledge of science." For that purpose, this book avoids "speculative theory and mathematical demonstrations" as much as possible.

{{< figure src="title page.jpg" title="Title page of the first edition" width="550" class="left">}}

The book is divided into dozens of little sections on topics such as mechanics, gravitation, simple machines, hydrostatics, electricity, magnetism, optics, and astronomy. Numerous illustrations, including many of instruments, accompany the text. The last few pages contain an explaination of terms used in the book.

This work is dedicated by Webster to John Bonnycastle (1751-1821), who is listed as a "Mathematical Master at the Royal Academy, Woolwich." Bonnycastle is best known for his many instructional texts on astronomy, algebra, geometry, and trigonometry, as well as for his translation of the French text *A General History of Mathematics*.
